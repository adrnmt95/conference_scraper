# Institutional Forbearance, Political Violence and Settlement Expansion

## Abstract

This illustrative research project examines government forbearance toward extremist
violence in the West Bank and its relationship to settlement expansion and political
accountability. The central question is when selective non-enforcement changes the
incentives of violent actors and the territorial consequences of their activities.
The proposed empirical approach uses difference-in-differences and event-study
designs to study political violence, enforcement and settlement outcomes. Relevant
audiences include political economy, conflict economics, institutional economics
and the study of state capacity. This example describes a research topic; it makes
no claim about empirical findings.
